1. Choose the right java.time type

TypeRepresentsExample use
LocalDateCalendar date, no time zoneContract expiration date
LocalTimeTime of day, no dateStore business hours
LocalDateTimeDate and time without zone or offsetSchedule local appointment time
InstantPoint on the UTC timelineAudit event timestamp
ZonedDateTimeDate/time tied to region-based zone rulesAppointment in America/Chicago
Duration / PeriodElapsed time / calendar-based amountTimeout / one month later

Do not assume a LocalDateTime identifies an instant. Daylight-saving transitions can make local times ambiguous or nonexistent.

2. Parse, format and convert dates

import java.time.*;
import java.time.format.DateTimeFormatter;

LocalDate due = LocalDate.parse("2026-10-12");
Instant created = Instant.now();
ZoneId zone = ZoneId.of("America/Chicago");
ZonedDateTime local = created.atZone(zone);
String formatted = local.format(DateTimeFormatter.ISO_ZONED_DATE_TIME);

Persist absolute event timestamps as instants or offsets consistently. Record business time zones explicitly when local-time interpretation matters.

3. Business calendar operations

LocalDate placed = LocalDate.of(2026, 10, 12);
LocalDate projected = placed.plusDays(5);
Period difference = Period.between(placed, projected);
Duration elapsed = Duration.between(Instant.now(), Instant.now().plusSeconds(90));

Calendar days are not business days. A production delivery-date calculator needs a maintained holiday calendar and locale-specific business rules.
