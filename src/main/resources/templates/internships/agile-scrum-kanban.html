1. Agile mindset and delivery principles

Agile is an approach to delivering value under uncertainty through short feedback loops, collaboration, and adaptation. The Agile Manifesto describes four values and twelve principles; it is not a prescribed meeting schedule.

Traditional predictive emphasisAgile / adaptive emphasis
Detailed upfront scope; controlled changeIterative discovery; frequent reprioritization
Phase-gate approvalsInspect working increments early
Measure conformance to planMeasure customer outcomes and useful delivery
Agile is not a license to skip architecture, security, testing, documentation or governance. Those practices belong in the definition of done.

2. Scrum framework: accountabilities and events

Scrum is a lightweight framework for complex work. A Scrum Team consists of a Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Developers. These are accountabilities, not necessarily corporate job titles.

ElementPurpose / ownership
Product GoalLong-term objective of the Scrum Team
Product BacklogOrdered, emergent work; Product Owner accountable for maximizing value and backlog management
Sprint (≤ 1 month)Timebox for delivering a useful, valuable Increment
Sprint PlanningAgree Sprint Goal, select work, and plan the approach
Daily Scrum (15 minutes)Developers inspect progress toward Sprint Goal and adapt the plan
Sprint ReviewInspect the outcome with stakeholders and adapt the Product Backlog
Sprint RetrospectiveImprove effectiveness, quality and working practices
Definition of DoneShared quality criteria an Increment must meet

Use the official Scrum Guide for normative wording. The Daily Scrum is not a status-report meeting to a manager.

3. Kanban and flow metrics

Kanban makes work visible, limits work in progress (WIP), and improves flow. A board should model how work actually moves—not merely count assigned tickets.

Backlog→Ready→In Progress (WIP 3)→Review (WIP 2)→Done
MetricMeaning
Lead timeTime from customer request to delivery
Cycle timeTime from start of active work to completion
ThroughputNumber of work items completed per unit time
WIPNumber of started but unfinished items
Work item ageElapsed time since an unfinished item started

Use explicit pull policies and visualize blocked tickets. Reducing WIP typically helps uncover bottlenecks; it is not a guarantee of faster delivery in every system.

4. Scrum versus Kanban: choosing a delivery approach

DimensionScrumKanban
CadenceFixed-length SprintsContinuous flow; cadences chosen by the team
Work planningSprint Goal and Sprint BacklogPull next ready item when capacity allows
RolesScrum accountabilities definedNo required role structure
Primary feedbackSprint events and incrementsFlow metrics, policies, service expectations
Works well whenA team can pursue a cohesive Sprint GoalRequests arrive continuously or priorities change often

Teams may combine practices, but should avoid claiming they are following Scrum while omitting core Scrum accountabilities and events.

5. End-to-end enterprise SDLC: backlog to production

Business need→Backlog refinement→Design / threat model→Code→Pull request / review→CI tests→Deploy→Monitor / learn

Quality gates should include peer review, unit/integration tests, secure dependency review, accessibility checks where relevant, operational telemetry, and rollback readiness.

  • Epic: Customer self-service order status.
  • User story: As a customer, I want to see my recent orders so that I can track fulfillment.
  • Acceptance criteria: Authorized customer sees only their own orders; no records return for unauthorized access; pagination and failure responses are tested.
  • Definition of Done: Reviewed, tested, security-checked, documented, deployable, and observable.

6. Practical Sprint planning, estimation, and refinement

Estimate for conversation and forecasting, not to score individuals. Story points (if used) are relative and team-specific. Never equate points to hours across teams.

ActivityExample outcome
Backlog refinementSplit oversized “manage all orders” epic into independently testable slices
Sprint planningGoal: enable secure order-list API; select capacity-appropriate work
Daily ScrumIdentify blocked migration and adapt collaborative plan
ReviewDemonstrate working secured endpoint to stakeholders
RetrospectiveAction: reduce review wait time using smaller pull requests
A Sprint should yield at least one usable Increment. Releasing that Increment to production is a business and operational decision, not a requirement that happens only at Sprint Review.

7. Hands-on lab: manage a two-week internship project

Scenario: Build a secured Order Tracking REST API

  1. Define one Product Goal and write an ordered backlog with at least eight user stories.
  2. Choose a Sprint Goal and identify a small deliverable Increment.
  3. Create a Kanban board with explicit workflow policies and WIP limits.
  4. Select acceptance criteria for authentication, authorization, pagination, errors, and tests.
  5. Simulate a blocked code-review item; describe how the team will respond.
  6. Deliver a brief Sprint Review demonstration and document one retrospective experiment.

Deliverables

Backlog CSV or Jira board screenshot, Sprint Goal, acceptance criteria, Kanban snapshot, reviewed pull request, test evidence, and retrospective action.
