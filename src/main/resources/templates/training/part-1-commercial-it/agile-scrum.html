How Commercial IT Works

Agile, Scrum, Sprints and Retrospectives

Understand why iterative delivery exists, how Scrum structures work, what happens during a Sprint, and how teams use feedback to improve both the product and the way they work.

Core mental model
Understand → Plan → Build → Verify → Review → Learn → Adapt. Agile reduces the cost of being wrong by shortening the feedback cycle.

Start with the delivery problem

Why do modern teams avoid planning an entire year of detailed software work upfront?

COMMERCIAL REALITY

A retailer wants a “Buy Online, Pick Up In Store” capability.

At the start, the organization does not know every edge case. Store inventory may be inaccurate, customers may not arrive, payment rules may change, operations may discover new constraints, and users may behave differently from what the team expected.

Requirements changeBusiness priorities, regulations, user needs and competitors move.
Complexity hides riskIntegration and production behavior expose problems documents cannot predict.
Feedback arrives lateA big-bang release can reveal months of wrong assumptions at once.
Value is delayedCustomers receive nothing while a huge release is still “in progress.”
Core idea: Agile delivery reduces the cost of being wrong by delivering, inspecting and adapting in smaller increments.
Agile mental model

Agile is not Scrum, Jira, stand-up meetings or “working faster”

Agile is a way of organizing knowledge work around collaboration, frequent delivery, feedback and adaptation. Scrum is one framework that puts those ideas into practice.

Step Activity What the team is trying to learn or accomplish
01UnderstandClarify the customer problem, business outcome, constraints and important unknowns.
02BuildCreate a small, integrated increment that can generate useful feedback.
03ValidateTest behavior with users, stakeholders, automated tests and production evidence.
04LearnCompare actual outcomes with the team's assumptions and expectations.
05AdaptChange product priorities, design choices or working practices based on new evidence.
NOT AGILE “We do not need requirements or architecture.”

Agile changes when and how detail is developed. It does not eliminate analysis, architecture, security, testing or documentation.

NOT AGILE “We release unfinished code every two weeks.”

An increment should meet the team's quality expectations. Iteration is not an excuse for knowingly poor engineering.

Scrum framework

Scrum gives a team a lightweight operating rhythm

Scrum organizes product development around a small team, a Product Goal, a prioritized Product Backlog and time-boxed Sprints that produce a usable Increment.

Category Scrum element Purpose
ACCOUNTABILITIES Product Owner Maximizes product value and is accountable for effective Product Backlog management.
Scrum MasterHelps the team and organization understand and use Scrum effectively through coaching, facilitation and impediment removal.
DevelopersCreate the Increment. “Developers” includes the skills necessary to deliver the product, not only programmers.
ARTIFACTS Product Backlog Ordered work needed to improve the product and support the Product Goal.
Sprint BacklogSprint Goal, selected Product Backlog Items and the Developers' evolving plan.
IncrementIntegrated, usable product value that satisfies the Definition of Done.
EVENTS Sprint Planning Establish why the Sprint is valuable, what can be done and how the work will begin.
Daily ScrumDevelopers inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt their plan.
Sprint ReviewScrum Team and stakeholders inspect the outcome and adapt future direction.
Sprint RetrospectiveThe Scrum Team improves quality, effectiveness and the way it works.
Inside a Sprint

Follow one feature from idea to usable increment

SPRINT EXAMPLE

“As a customer, I want to cancel an eligible order before shipment.”

The same requirement used in earlier lessons now becomes a piece of planned delivery work.

Step Sprint activity What happens Typical output
01RefineClarify rules, dependencies, acceptance criteria and technical unknowns.Better-understood backlog items.
02PlanSelect work supporting the Sprint Goal and establish an initial delivery plan.Sprint Goal + Sprint Backlog.
03BuildImplement UI, API, service, database, security and integration changes.Integrated working software.
04VerifyUse unit, integration, functional, security and regression tests to produce evidence.Quality evidence and defect findings.
05ReviewStakeholders inspect the Increment and discuss changes in the business environment.Product feedback and backlog adaptation.
06ImproveThe team identifies a concrete process or engineering improvement.Retrospective action for the next Sprint.
Important: A Sprint is not merely a two-week project plan. It is a short learning and delivery cycle centered on a Sprint Goal and a usable Increment.
Scrum events

What actually happens in the meetings?

Each Scrum event exists to create transparency, inspection and adaptation—not simply to fill a calendar.

Event Purpose What happens Key question / output
PLANSprint PlanningBegins the SprintThe Scrum Team creates the Sprint Goal, selects backlog items and develops enough of a plan to begin delivering.Why is this Sprint valuable? What can we achieve? How will we start?
DAILYDaily Scrum15-minute event for DevelopersInspect progress toward the Sprint Goal, expose coordination needs and adapt today's plan.A better plan for the next day of work.
REVIEWSprint ReviewInspect product outcomeScrum Team and stakeholders inspect what was accomplished and adapt future product direction.What changed? What did we learn? What should happen next?
RETROSprint RetrospectiveImprove the working systemThe Scrum Team examines quality, collaboration, tools, process and impediments.One or more practical improvements for future Sprints.
Artifacts and commitments

The three things teams continuously make transparent

Scrum artifacts represent work or value. Each artifact has a commitment that helps create focus and transparency.

Artifact Commitment What it represents Question it should answer
Product BacklogProduct GoalEverything currently known to be needed for the product, ordered to support learning and value.Where are we trying to take the product?
Sprint BacklogSprint GoalThe current Sprint objective, selected backlog items and an evolving delivery plan.What meaningful objective are we pursuing now?
IncrementDefinition of DoneIntegrated work that satisfies the team's agreed quality bar and is usable.What evidence tells us this work is genuinely complete?
Backlog and user stories

A backlog is a decision system, not a dumping ground

Teams often describe product needs using user stories, but the story format is optional. What matters is shared understanding of value, constraints and acceptance.

EXAMPLE USER STORY
As a customer, I want to cancel an eligible order before fulfillment starts, so that I can correct a purchase without contacting support.
Business rules

Which statuses are cancellable? Is there a cutoff time? What happens to inventory and payment?

Acceptance criteria

Observable conditions proving expected behavior, including denial and failure scenarios.

Engineering work

API, service, persistence, UI, tests, security, telemetry and deployment changes required to satisfy the outcome.

Estimation and forecasting

Story points are not hours and velocity is not employee productivity

Estimation is useful when it supports planning and learning. It becomes harmful when uncertain forecasts are treated as precise promises or performance scores.

Concept How to understand it Common misuse to avoid
Relative estimationCompare complexity, effort, uncertainty and risk rather than pretending every task can be predicted exactly.Treating points as a disguised hour conversion.
VelocityA team-specific historical planning signal that may help with local forecasting.Comparing teams or using velocity as an employee performance target.
CapacityActual team availability after leave, incidents, support work, training and other obligations.Assuming every Sprint has identical available effort.
ForecastAn informed expectation that should change when new evidence arrives.Treating a forecast as an unconditional guarantee.
Cross-functional collaboration

Scrum does not erase the roles from Lesson 3

Commercial organizations still need product, architecture, security, QA, data, DevOps and operational expertise. The goal is to bring those capabilities into delivery early instead of turning each specialty into a late-stage queue.

Capability Contribution during iterative delivery
ProductClarifies value, priority, user outcomes and acceptance intent.
ArchitectureGuides boundaries, non-functional requirements, integrations and consequential trade-offs.
Engineering + QABuild and verify the Increment continuously rather than separating coding and testing into isolated phases.
SecurityBrings threat analysis, authorization and control requirements into design and implementation early.
DevOps / SREProvide delivery automation, observability, reliability practices and production feedback.
Mature behavior: Bring specialized knowledge into the Sprint early. Do not wait until the end for an “architecture approval,” “security approval,” or “QA phase” if those risks can be addressed continuously.

Example Sprint Goal

Customers can safely cancel eligible orders without contacting support.

Definition of Done example

Business rules implemented, authorization enforced, automated tests passing, UI integrated, logging present, code reviewed and application deployable.

Common anti-patterns

Teams can perform every Scrum ceremony and still not be Agile

ANTI-PATTERNDaily Scrum becomes manager status reporting

Developers speak to the manager one-by-one rather than coordinating with each other around the Sprint Goal.

ANTI-PATTERNEverything is “90% done” at Sprint end

Work is started in parallel but not integrated, tested and completed. Flow matters more than utilization.

ANTI-PATTERNRetrospective produces no change

The same complaints repeat every Sprint because nobody chooses an owner, experiment or measurable improvement.

ANTI-PATTERNVelocity becomes a target

Once story points are treated as performance, estimates inflate and the metric loses forecasting value.

Hands-on lab

Create a mini Sprint for the E-Commerce reference application

Use the existing project. Do not invent ten features. Plan one small, coherent outcome that could realistically be demonstrated.

1
Choose one product outcome

Example: enable a customer to cancel an order only when business rules permit it.

2
Write one Sprint Goal

Describe the outcome, not a list of technical tasks.

3
Create 3–5 backlog items

Include business behavior, security/testing work and any UI/API changes needed.

4
Write acceptance criteria

Include at least one successful path and two failure/denial paths.

5
Define Done

State the quality evidence required before you would call the increment usable.

6
Write one retrospective improvement

Assume the Sprint exposed a problem. Define one concrete change for the next Sprint.

Deliverable: Sprint Goal → Backlog Items → Acceptance Criteria → Definition of Done → Review Questions → One Retrospective Improvement.

Career perspective

Commercial engineers are expected to participate in delivery, not only coding

A strong engineer can discuss a story with Product, identify architectural risk, collaborate with QA and Security, review peers' work, demonstrate an Increment and learn from production feedback.

Stage Typical question What is developing
01“What ticket should I code?”Learning implementation mechanics and team delivery practices.
02“What outcome is this story trying to achieve?”Connecting code to product intent and acceptance criteria.
03“What risks could prevent this Sprint Goal?”Reasoning about architecture, security, dependencies, uncertainty and quality.
04“How can the team improve flow and product outcomes?”Thinking systemically about delivery, feedback, collaboration and continuous improvement.
