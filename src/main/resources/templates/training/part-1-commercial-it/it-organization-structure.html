How Commercial IT Works

IT Organization Structure

Software engineering does not happen in isolation. Learn how business, product, architecture, engineering, security, data, quality, platform, operations and support work together to deliver a real business capability.

The mental model for this lesson
Business need → Product decision → Architecture → Build → Secure & test → Deploy → Operate → Improve

Start with the business

One button can involve an entire organization

Imagine that an e-commerce company asks for one apparently simple feature:

BUSINESS REQUEST
“Allow a customer to cancel an order before it ships.”
Who builds this? The answer is not simply “a Java developer.”
Business rules

When is cancellation allowed? What happens to payment, inventory and customer communication?

Technology

UI, API, service logic, database transactions and downstream integrations must work together.

Operational controls

Identity, authorization, testing, deployment, logging, monitoring and support must be considered.

Core idea: Commercial IT converts business outcomes into technology capabilities that can be operated safely and reliably.
Organization mental model

The major functions in a commercial IT organization

This is a capability map, not a mandatory reporting hierarchy. A small company may combine several functions into one team; a global enterprise may have many teams for each function.

Sequence Commercial IT function Primary purpose Typical responsibilities
01
Business Leadership & Domain Teams Where the business need begins
Define the outcome the organization needs to achieve. Business goals, customer needs, operating policy, budget, domain rules and measurable outcomes.
02
Product Management Turns business goals into prioritized capabilities
Decide what should be built, why it matters and when it should be delivered. Roadmap, backlog, requirements, prioritization, user stories, acceptance criteria and stakeholder alignment.
03
Enterprise / Solution / Application Architecture Connects business intent to a coherent technical design
Determine how applications, data, security, integrations and infrastructure should fit together. Solution structure, boundaries, standards, integration decisions, non-functional requirements, risks and trade-offs.
04
Software & Data Engineering Builds the working technology capability
Implement the approved design as usable, testable software and data services.
UI Engineering API & Service Engineering Data Engineering Integrations Automated Tests
05
Quality, Security, Platform & DevOps Makes the solution safe, repeatable and production-ready
Validate quality, enforce security controls and provide reliable delivery/runtime foundations.
Quality Engineering Security / IAM CI/CD Cloud / Platform Infrastructure Automation
06
Operations / SRE Keeps production healthy and dependable
Operate the solution reliably after deployment. Monitoring, availability, SLOs, logging, alerting, incident response, capacity, resilience and recovery.
07
Production Systems & Users Where business value is actually realized
Serve real customers, employees and business processes. Real transactions, real data, revenue impact, customer experience and operational business outcomes.
08
Service Desk / Support / Incident Management Closes the feedback and recovery loop
Help users, restore service and feed operational learning back into improvement. User support, ticket triage, escalation, incident coordination, recovery, knowledge articles and feedback.
This sequence is a flow of responsibility—not a rigid reporting hierarchy.
Real delivery is iterative. Operations, Support, Security, Architecture, Product and Engineering continuously feed information back into earlier stages.
Architectural perspective: Architecture is deliberately shown between business/product intent and implementation. Architects collaborate horizontally across engineering, security, data, infrastructure and operations; they are not merely people who draw diagrams.

End-to-end workflow

Follow “Cancel Order” from idea to production

This is the commercial context behind the code you will eventually write.

1
Business defines the policy

Cancellation is allowed only before shipment. The business also decides refund and inventory implications.

2
Product makes it implementable

A Product Manager or Product Owner creates/refines the story, priority and acceptance criteria with stakeholders.

3
Architecture identifies affected components

Browser, Spring MVC, REST API, service layer, MySQL, Keycloak/Spring Security and any payment or fulfillment integration are considered.

4
Engineering implements the capability

Developers change UI/API contracts, business rules, persistence logic, integration behavior and automated tests.

5
Security and quality validate critical behavior

A customer must not cancel another customer's order. Invalid order states must be rejected. Regression tests protect existing behavior.

6
CI/CD builds and deploys

The change is compiled, tested, scanned, packaged and promoted through controlled environments.

7
Production serves real traffic

The feature now affects real customers, transactions, revenue and business operations.

8
Operations observes and improves

Logs, metrics, traces, alerts, incidents and user feedback reveal whether the capability is healthy and useful.

APPLICATION REQUEST FLOW

How a browser request reaches the database

Step Application layer What happens here
01
Browser / Thymeleaf UI User-facing presentation layer
The user views pages, submits forms, clicks buttons and initiates HTTP requests.
02
Controller / REST Controller HTTP entry point
Receives the HTTP request, validates request-level input and delegates work to the service layer.
03
Service Layer Business logic
Applies business rules, coordinates operations and controls application behavior.
04
Repository / JPA Persistence abstraction
Maps Java entities to database operations and handles persistence through Spring Data JPA.
05
MySQL 8 Persistent data store
Stores customers, products, orders and other durable transactional application data.
SECURITY REQUEST FLOW

How authentication and authorization protect the application

Step Security component Responsibility
01
Browser User starts the request
The user attempts to access a protected application page or API.
02
Keycloak Identity Provider
Authenticates the user and issues trusted OAuth 2.0 / OpenID Connect security tokens.
03
Spring Security Application security boundary
Validates identity, evaluates roles/authorities and enforces access-control rules.
04
Authorized Application Request Protected business processing
The request reaches application logic only after required security checks succeed.

Small team or individualMultiple specialized teams and stakeholders
Short project lifetimeSystems may evolve for years or decades
Limited users and test dataReal users, money, data and business commitments

Your first engineering job

A realistic day is more than writing code

The exact schedule varies by company and team, but commercial software engineering is collaborative, evidence-driven and spread across design, development, review, testing and delivery activities.

Time Activity What you are doing
9:00 AM
Daily stand-up
Share yesterday's progress, today's plan and anything blocking delivery.
9:30 AM
Review Jira story
Read the business requirement, acceptance criteria, dependencies and expected behavior before coding.
10:00 AM
Design discussion
Discuss API contracts, application boundaries, data changes, security concerns and integration dependencies.
11:00 AM
Implement
Write production code, unit tests and supporting configuration while following team standards.
1:30 PM
Pull request
Submit changes for peer review, explain important decisions and respond to reviewer feedback.
2:30 PM
Integration work
Resolve API, database, environment, authentication or downstream-system integration issues.
3:30 PM
Work with QA
Validate acceptance criteria, reproduce defects, inspect evidence and confirm corrected behavior.
4:30 PM
Check the delivery pipeline
Review build, automated tests, security scans and deployment status before concluding the workday.
What beginners should notice

Only part of the day is spent typing code. Commercial engineering also requires communication, requirements analysis, design, peer review, integration, testing and operational awareness.

Hands-on lab

Trace one request through the reference application

Your goal is not to change code yet. Your goal is to discover how the layers connect.

Open the E-Commerce Reference menu.

4
Follow the service layer.

Common beginner misconceptions

Correct these mental models early

MYTH
“The developer owns the entire application.”

Commercial systems normally have shared ownership across product, engineering, security, platform and operations.

MYTH
“Architecture means drawing diagrams.”

Architecture is decision-making about structure, constraints, trade-offs and qualities that materially affect the system.

MYTH
“QA happens after development.”

Quality is designed and tested continuously; late testing alone is an expensive way to discover defects.

MYTH
“Production is just another server.”

Production requires operational ownership, monitoring, controlled access, resilience, recovery and change discipline.
