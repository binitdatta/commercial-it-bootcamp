How Commercial IT Works

Roles and Responsibilities in an IT Shop

Learn who defines business value, who prioritizes work, who designs the solution, who builds and verifies it, who secures and deploys it, and who keeps it reliable in production.

Core mental model
Business defines value → Product defines what → Architecture shapes how → Engineering builds → Quality/Security verify → Platform/SRE operate → Support closes the loop.
Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to

1
Separate accountability from activityUnderstand who owns an outcome versus who contributes to the work.
2
Differentiate major IT rolesProduct, architecture, engineering, QA, security, DevOps, SRE, data and support.
3
Follow collaboration across deliverySee how one requirement moves through many roles before it reaches production.
4
Recognize organization variantsTitles differ by company; responsibilities and decision boundaries matter more than labels.
Start with a real commercial problem

One feature can involve a dozen roles

BUSINESS REQUEST

“Allow an authenticated customer to cancel an order before shipment begins.”

This sounds like a small button on a web page. In a commercial system it can affect policy, UX, APIs, database state, authorization, inventory, payment/refund processing, audit logs, testing, deployment, monitoring and customer support.

Core idea: Commercial software delivery is collaborative because no single role has enough authority, context and expertise to own every decision safely.
Role map

The major responsibility groups in a commercial IT shop

This is a responsibility map, not a mandatory reporting hierarchy. In a startup one person may wear several hats; in a global enterprise each row may represent multiple specialized teams.

Group Responsibility area Typical roles Primary contribution
01 Business & ProductWhy and what Business Sponsor · Product Manager · Product Owner · Business Analyst · UX Define business outcomes, customer value, priorities, requirements and acceptance intent.
02 ArchitectureHow the solution fits together Enterprise Architect · Solution Architect · Application Architect · Data Architect · Security Architect Shape boundaries, integrations, standards, non-functional requirements, risks and trade-offs.
03 Build & VerifyCreate and prove behavior Frontend · Backend · Full Stack · QA / Test Automation · Integration · Data Engineering Implement working software, data movement, integrations, automated tests and defect fixes.
04 Secure, Deliver & OperateProduction readiness Security Engineering · DevOps / Platform · Cloud Engineering · SRE · Operations · Support Protect, deploy, observe, recover and continuously improve production services.
05 Leadership & GovernanceCoordination and accountability Engineering Manager · Tech Lead · Project / Program Management · Compliance · Risk · Vendor Management Coordinate people, decisions, delivery, governance, risk, evidence and organizational capability.
Key point: Learn the decision boundary and accountability behind a title. Titles vary dramatically between organizations; the underlying responsibilities are more stable.
Business and product roles

Who decides why and what?

These roles turn business needs into prioritized, understandable work for delivery teams.

Role Primary accountability What the role does Typically owns
BS Business Sponsor / StakeholderOwns the business outcome Defines the problem, desired outcome, business constraints and funding context. Business value, policy intent and business-level priority.
PM Product ManagerOwns product direction Translates business goals and customer needs into product strategy, roadmap and prioritized outcomes. Roadmap, product outcomes, prioritization and customer value.
PO Product OwnerOwns the near-term backlog Clarifies stories, acceptance criteria and ordering of work for a delivery team. Backlog clarity, story priority and acceptance intent.
BA Business AnalystConnects process and requirements Studies workflows, rules, exceptions, data and stakeholder needs, especially in complex or regulated domains. Requirement analysis, process detail and traceability support.

In some organizations Product Manager and Product Owner are separate roles; in others one person performs both responsibilities.

Architecture roles

Who determines how the solution should fit together?

Architecture operates at different scopes. The distinction is primarily about breadth of responsibility, not about who is “more technical.”

Scope Architecture role Main focus Typical decisions
01 Enterprise Architect Enterprise capabilities, portfolio direction, standards, roadmaps and cross-domain alignment. Platform strategy, application rationalization, enterprise patterns, capability roadmaps.
02 Solution Architect End-to-end solution across applications, integrations, data, security and infrastructure. System boundaries, integration patterns, NFRs, dependencies, major technology trade-offs.
03 Application Architect Internal application structure, frameworks, patterns, APIs and code-level boundaries. Layering, framework conventions, API contracts, module boundaries, implementation patterns.
04 Data Architect Data models, ownership, movement, lineage and data-platform patterns. System of record, schemas, integration models, governance, analytical architecture.
05 Security Architect Identity, trust boundaries, authentication, authorization, encryption and threat controls. IAM patterns, trust models, security controls, privileged access, compliance architecture.
Important: Architecture is not “the person who draws diagrams.” Architecture is the discipline of making consequential design decisions and trade-offs before those choices become expensive to reverse.
Engineering roles

Who builds the software?

Frontend Engineer

Browser/mobile UI, state management, accessibility, client-side validation, API integration.

Typical: Angular, React, Vue
Backend Engineer

APIs, services, domain logic, persistence, messaging, integrations and security enforcement.

Typical: Java/Spring, .NET, Python
Full-Stack Engineer

Works across both UI and backend boundaries, usually with deeper strength in one area.

Broad delivery ownership
Integration Engineer

Connects SaaS, ERP, APIs, files, queues and enterprise systems reliably.

APIs · Events · ETL · iPaaS
Data Engineer

Builds pipelines, transformations and reliable data movement into operational and analytical platforms.

Batch · Streaming · Lakehouse
Tech Lead

Provides technical direction to a team, reviews design/code and coordinates implementation decisions.

Leadership without necessarily being a manager
Quality and security

Who proves the system is safe enough to release?

QA

QA / Quality Engineer

Verifies behavior and quality

Designs test scenarios, automation, regression suites and non-functional tests. Good QA challenges assumptions before defects reach production.

Focus: correctness, regression, testability, evidence.
SEC

Security Engineer

Reduces technical security risk

Works on IAM, secure coding, vulnerability management, secrets, application security, threat detection and security tooling.

Focus: confidentiality, integrity, availability, abuse resistance.
Shared responsibility: QA does not “own quality” alone and Security does not “own security” alone. Developers, architects, product teams and operators all contribute to both.

Accountability model

RACI: a simple way to clarify who does what

RACI is useful when many teams contribute to the same outcome and ownership needs to be explicit.

Code Meaning What it means in practice
RResponsiblePerforms or coordinates the work required to complete the activity.
AAccountableUltimately owns the outcome and has final accountability for the result.
CConsultedProvides expertise or input before the decision or activity is completed.
IInformedNeeds awareness of progress, decisions or outcomes but does not perform the activity.

RACI is not required for every story. It is most useful when ownership is ambiguous, many teams are involved, or regulated/operational responsibilities must be explicit.

A day in the life

What collaboration looks like for a new software engineer

A commercial engineering day mixes coding with product clarification, design, review, testing and operational learning.

Time Activity Who you collaborate with / what you learn
9:00 AMStand-upCoordinate progress, plan and blockers with engineering, QA and product.
9:30 AMStory reviewClarify acceptance criteria and business intent with the Product Owner.
10:30 AMDesign discussionConfirm API, data and technical decisions with senior engineers or architects.
11:30 AMImplementationWrite production code and unit tests.
2:00 PMPull requestReceive code-review feedback and discuss implementation trade-offs.
3:00 PMQA collaborationReproduce an edge-case defect and compare behavior with acceptance criteria.
4:00 PMDelivery pipelineReview build, test, scan and deployment results.
4:30 PMProduction learningReview logs, metrics or a recent incident to understand real system behavior.
Beginner takeaway

Writing code is essential, but commercial engineering success also depends on collaboration, evidence and operational awareness.

Common misconceptions

What beginners often get wrong

MISCONCEPTION“The developer owns the whole application.”

Developers own important implementation decisions, but business, architecture, security, operations and product responsibilities remain distinct.

MISCONCEPTION“Architects just create PowerPoint diagrams.”

Effective architects make and govern high-impact technical decisions, resolve trade-offs and keep solutions coherent across boundaries.

MISCONCEPTION“QA happens after coding is finished.”

Quality engineering should influence acceptance criteria, testability, automation and risk throughout the delivery lifecycle.

MISCONCEPTION“DevOps means the deployment team.”

DevOps is a delivery culture and set of practices. Some companies use DevOps Engineer as a role title, but the concept is broader.

Career perspective

Strong engineers learn to work across role boundaries

You do not need to become every role. You do need to understand what the people around you are responsible for.

Stage Typical career position How collaboration usually expands
01Graduate EngineerLearn the codebase, team process, terminology, quality standards and how assigned work fits into a larger product.
02Software EngineerOwn features more independently and collaborate directly with product, QA and peer engineers.
03Senior EngineerReason across components, mentor others and influence design, operational quality and cross-team decisions.
04Tech LeadCoordinate technical direction, implementation decisions and delivery across a team or workstream.
05Architect / Engineering Manager / SpecialistOperate across broader organizational boundaries using technical depth, business understanding, communication and judgment.

Seniority increasingly requires combining technical depth with business understanding, communication, judgment and the ability to coordinate decisions across disciplines.
